Sancta — iPhone app

Last updated: 25 November 2026

This policy explains what personal data the Sancta app processes, why, who receives it and what your rights are. Sancta was previously called FaithFlow: some technical names, such as this web address and our email address, still use the old name.

In short

  • We use your data only to run Sancta. We don’t sell it and we don’t track you across other apps or websites. The free version shows ads only if you say yes, and they are never personalised; Premium has none.
  • Other users can’t see your tradition, moods, notes or reflections, or what your acts of kindness are. They can see your username, streak and XP, and your friends also see your Verse Wall posts.
  • Data about your faith and wellbeing is “special category” data under the GDPR: we process it only with your explicit consent.
  • The AI Guide is optional. If you choose to use it, what you write to it is sent to DeepSeek, in China, with the risks explained in section 6.
  • You can delete your account and data at any time: Profile → Settings → Delete account.

3What other users can see

  • Any signed-in Sancta user can see your profile: your username, streak, best streak and XP, and the random account ID that identifies it. In the app, someone who searches for your exact username finds you. If you’d rather not be identifiable, choose a username that isn’t your real name.
  • Your friends also see your Verse Wall posts and your place in the friends’ leaderboard. With the Share button they can share the text of your posts outside Sancta, without your name.
  • People in a challenge with you, and the friends invited to it, see your username and your progress. Progress is currently your overall total for the challenge’s measure — XP, chapters read, or the number of acts of kindness you have logged (never what they are) — or, for a daily-tasks challenge, how many of today’s daily tasks you have completed (not which ones).
  • If you block someone, you and that person can no longer see each other’s profile, Verse Wall posts or challenge progress, find each other, send each other friend requests or invite each other to challenges, and you are no longer friends. The person isn’t notified. You can unblock them in Profile → Settings → Blocked users.
  • Your email address, tradition, moods, notes, reflections, the content of your acts of kindness and your Guide conversations are never shown to other users.
  • Sharing is up to you: when you share a verse, it goes only where you send it with the iPhone share sheet.

4Where your data is kept and for how long

On your iPhone. The app keeps a copy of your data on the device so it also works offline, and stores what the widget and the daily notifications need (such as your streak, the saint or verse of the day, your language and tradition). Notifications are scheduled by the iPhone itself: we don’t use push-notification servers.

On our servers. Account and app data are stored in our database, hosted by Supabase in Frankfurt, Germany (EU); our server functions, which relay Guide messages and voice requests, are set to run there too.

How long. We keep your data for as long as you have an account. When you delete your account, we delete the account and the data linked to it from our database; copies may remain in backups for up to 30 days before they are overwritten. The app also clears the progress, notes and moods stored on that iPhone (settings such as language and tradition remain until you delete the app). Server logs are deleted automatically within 28 days. We keep emails you send us, reports (also after the accounts concerned are deleted) and our moderation decisions for as long as we need them to handle the matter and at most 2 years after it is closed, unless they are needed for a legal claim. RevenueCat keeps the purchase history linked to the random purchase ID for as long as we use its service; deleting your Sancta account doesn’t delete it, so write to us if you want it deleted. Apple keeps its own purchase records under its privacy policy. We cannot tell you how long DeepSeek keeps data (section 6).

6The AI Guide and DeepSeek (China)

The Sancta Guide and the monthly AI reflection are generated by DeepSeek, an artificial-intelligence company based in the People’s Republic of China. Using them is optional: the rest of Sancta works without them.

What is sent. The messages of your current Guide conversation (the whole conversation is sent with each new message, so that the Guide can follow it), including the messages the app writes for you when you tap a suggestion, “Tell me more about this feeling” or the check-in card on Home (they can state your mood or a theme recorded earlier, such as anxiety or health), and — if you ask for a monthly reflection — the acts of kindness you logged that month. They pass through our server, which does not add your email, username or account ID. Sancta does not save your Guide conversations: they are lost when the app is closed (only the one-word themes described in section 2 are saved).

The risks. China has no EU adequacy decision, and DeepSeek offers us neither the EU Standard Contractual Clauses nor a data processing agreement: no contract between us and DeepSeek protects your messages. DeepSeek’s privacy policy does not cover people who use apps built on its platform, such as Sancta, and its terms don’t say how long it keeps what it receives or whether it uses it to improve its AI, so we cannot tell you. Chinese authorities may be able to access your messages; there may be no independent data protection authority there, and your GDPR rights may not be applied or enforceable against DeepSeek. In January 2025 Italy’s data protection authority (the Garante) ordered DeepSeek to limit the processing of Italian users’ data by its own chatbot service. What you write may reveal your religious beliefs or your health.

Your choice. We send this data only with your explicit consent, which the app asks for before your first message or reflection (Art. 6(1)(a), Art. 9(2)(a) and Art. 49(1)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw it at any time in Profile → Settings → AI Guide; from then on none of your messages or acts of kindness are sent to DeepSeek (the saint facts, which contain no personal data, are still requested). Withdrawing does not affect what was sent before.

Our advice. Don’t write names, contact details, or private information about yourself or other people.

7Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to: access your data and receive a copy; have it corrected; have it deleted; restrict its processing; and receive it in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format (portability). You can withdraw any consent at any time, without affecting the processing carried out before. Exercising your rights is free of charge.

Right to object. You can object at any time, on grounds relating to your particular situation, to processing based on our legitimate interests (for example server logs or answering your emails). We will then stop, unless we have compelling legitimate grounds or need the data to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.

Some things you can do directly in the app: for example, delete highlights and notes, delete your Verse Wall posts, leave challenges, remove friends, block people, turn off the AI Guide, turn off ads and delete your account.

To exercise your rights, email faithflowsupport@gmail.com from the address linked to your account (or tell us how we can confirm it’s you). We reply within one month; for complex requests this may be extended by two more months, and we will tell you why.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection authority: in Italy, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali (www.garanteprivacy.it), or the authority of the EU country where you live, work or where you believe the infringement took place.

8Security

Data travels encrypted (HTTPS). Our database applies access rules so that each account can read only the data it is allowed to see; the secret keys of the paid external services we use, such as AI and voice, stay on our server, not in the app; passwords are stored only in hashed form.

No system is completely secure. If a personal data breach may put your rights at risk, we will notify the competent supervisory authority and, where required, you, as the law requires.

9Age limit

Sancta is only for people aged 18 or over. We do not knowingly process data of younger people. If you believe a minor has created an account, write to us and we will delete it.

10Automated decisions and profiling

The app uses your recent moods and the themes it recognises in your Guide messages (for example “anxiety”) to choose verses and occasional check-in questions. This is a simple form of profiling: a keyword match done on your iPhone; the themes are saved with your account so that they also work on your other devices, and they are deleted with it. We do not make decisions about you based solely on automated processing that produce legal or similarly significant effects, and you can ignore the suggestions.

We ask first. No ad is shown, and nothing is sent to Google for advertising, until you say yes. EEA, United Kingdom and Switzerland. If you say yes, Google’s consent window then appears (Google’s consent management platform, certified under the IAB Europe Transparency & Consent Framework). Ads are requested only if you also consent there to storing and accessing information on your device and to Google as an ad partner. If you refuse consent there, Sancta requests no ads at all. To show this window, the app contacts Google’s servers; no ad is requested at that point. You can review or change these choices in Profile → Settings → Google ad privacy choices.

Who provides the ads. The ads are served by Google through its AdMob service, using Google’s Mobile Ads software (SDK) built into the app. For its processing of the data it receives through the SDK, Google acts as an independent controller under the Google Ads Controller-Controller Data Protection Terms, and Google’s Privacy Policy applies: policies.google.com/privacy; see also how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Never personalized. We have set the SDK to request only non-personalized ads: they are not based on your past behaviour or on a profile of you, but on context, such as the app and your approximate location (for example, your city). We have also switched on Google’s restricted data processing, switched off Google’s optional “publisher first-party ID”, and asked for ads suitable for general audiences only. Sancta does not ask for permission to track you (Apple’s App Tracking Transparency), so the SDK cannot read your device’s advertising identifier (IDFA).

What Google receives. When the app requests or shows an ad, the SDK sends Google: your IP address (from which an approximate location can be estimated); technical information about your device and the app (for example device model, operating system and version, language, app version, and an identifier limited to apps of the same developer); information about the ads shown and your interactions with them (for example views and taps); and crash and performance diagnostics. Non-personalized ads still use device identifiers or similar on-device storage for frequency capping (limiting how often you see the same ad) and aggregated ad reporting, and Google uses the data to prevent fraud and abuse and to maintain and improve its services. Because the request comes from Sancta, Google can tell that your device uses a Christian app, which may reveal your religious beliefs. This is why we ask for your explicit consent.

What Google never receives from us. Sancta never passes to Google, or to any advertiser, your name, email address or account ID, your prayers, intentions, notes or journal entries, your conversations with the AI Guide, your reading history, your friends, or anything else you write or do in the app. We do not sell your personal data.

Legal basis. Your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR) and, because ad requests may reveal your religious beliefs, your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR), given in the app before any ad. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, storing and accessing information on your device also relies on your consent in Google’s consent window (art. 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive, implemented in Italy by art. 122 of the Privacy Code, and the equivalent UK and Swiss rules). You can withdraw consent at any time, as easily as you gave it; this does not affect processing carried out before you withdrew it.

Transfers and retention. Google may process this data outside the EEA, including in the United States. Google LLC is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, its UK Extension and the Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, and uses standard contractual clauses where required. Google keeps the data it receives according to its own retention policies (policies.google.com/technologies/retention). Your answers to the ads question are kept with your account until you delete it.
